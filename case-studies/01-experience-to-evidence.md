# Live show continuity and cross-department escalation

**Evidence classification: AUTHOR-REPORTED EXPERIENCE + ORIGINAL DEMONSTRATION DESIGN. Not independently verified employer documentation.**

## Situation and scope
Reported situation: multiple cruise productions with timecoded show operation, rehearsals, cue changes, technical handovers and performance-readiness decisions. Reports of stressful team interactions should be translated into process design, not public accusations.

## Method and technical decisions
Workflow: (1) establish show readiness criteria; (2) ask each department for status; (3) distinguish safety-critical, show-critical and cosmetic issues; (4) document the impact and escalation owner; (5) communicate in observable facts without attributing motives; (6) obtain go/no-go authority; (7) record recovery and handover.

## Functional architecture (illustrative)
```text
[Department checks] --> [Stage/technical readiness] --> [Go/no-go decision] --> [Show] --> [Handover]
```

## Leadership and management transfer
The operational lesson is to make failure modes observable, separate evidence from assumptions, prioritize safe show continuity, define responsibility and preserve a clear handover. These are transferable to fleet-level standardization, but **do not establish prior fleet-management authority**.
